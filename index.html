For switching to fentanyl patch, the assistant uses the product-literature opioid-rotation table by default because FUKKM directs prescribers to previous 24-hour opioid requirement/product literature and lists 90 mg oral morphine/24 h as the 25 mcg/h anchor.

Formula & source hierarchy

The local MOH ratios intentionally take precedence where they differ from international tables. For example, this assistant uses oral morphine:IV morphine 2.5:1 and oral tramadol:oral morphine 5:1 from the MOH Malaysia Pain Management Handbook.

Switching between different opioids

For a different non-patch opioid, the assistant displays the calculated equianalgesic target dose and a separate MOH starting range of 50–67% of that value (approximately a 33–50% reduction) to account for incomplete cross-tolerance. It does not apply this reduction when changing route but keeping the same opioid.

FPM advises an individualised reduction of around 25–50% in most opioid switches and at least 50% in high-dose (around ≥500 mg oral morphine equivalent/24 h), elderly/frail, or toxicity-prone situations. Results at high OME therefore generate an additional warning.

Patches

Fentanyl patch: switching to a patch uses a product-literature starting-dose table rather than percentage-rounding a calculated patch strength. The default is the more conservative opioid-rotation/clinically-less-stable table. FUKKM specifically states that previous 24-hour opioid requirement/product literature should guide starting dose and lists oral morphine 90 mg/24 h as the 25 mcg/h reference point.

Buprenorphine patch: switching to a patch uses the discrete MOH Malaysia table. If the calculated OME falls between table values, the assistant shows the bracketing reference strengths and does not automatically round up or down.

Reverse conversion from fentanyl patch is shown as an approximate source-table range. Reverse conversion from buprenorphine patch to a full agonist is deliberately restricted to a reference OME because buprenorphine's high receptor affinity/partial agonism makes a simple symmetric conversion potentially misleading.

Total OME

Total OME adds the approximate oral-morphine-equivalent contribution of each entered opioid. It is useful for comparing overall opioid burden, but the total OME must not be used directly as the dose of a replacement opioid. CDC specifically cautions against using MME/OME totals as a direct opioid-switching dose.

Excluded / specialist conversions

Methadone, neuraxial opioids, intrathecal/epidural conversion and remifentanil conversion are intentionally excluded from automatic switching because their kinetics/potency relationships require a separate specialist approach.

Body size

BMI and dosing-weight estimates

Body Mass Index (BMI)
BMI = kg/m²
Formula, clinical notes & references

Formula: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height² (m²).

BMI is a practical screening measure, not a direct measure of adiposity. Adult interpretation should account for age, muscle mass and ethnicity; paediatric BMI requires age- and sex-specific centiles/z-scores rather than adult cut-offs.

WHO — Obesity and overweight · NICE NG246 — BMI interpretation

Ideal & Adjusted Body Weight (IBW / AjBW)
Enter sex and height to calculate IBW.
Formula, clinical notes & references

Devine IBW: male = 50 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 ft; female = 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 ft.

Adjusted body weight used here: AjBW = IBW + 0.4 × (actual body weight − IBW). AjBW is calculated only when an actual body weight above IBW is entered.

IBW is a historical dosing scalar rather than a direct measurement of lean mass. The 0.4 AjBW correction is an empirical convention used for selected drugs in obesity, not a universal dosing weight. Drug-specific obesity guidance, pharmacokinetic monitoring and local policy take precedence. Interpret Devine-derived values cautiously below 152.4 cm because the original convention is based on height above 5 ft.

Pai & Paloucek — Origin of ideal body weight equations · Pai — Weight-based drug dosing limitations in obesity

Renal

CrCl and current race-free eGFR estimates

Creatinine Clearance — Cockcroft–Gault
CrCl mL/min
Formula, clinical notes & references

Formula: male CrCl = (140 − age) × weight(kg) ÷ [72 × SCr(mg/dL)]; female result × 0.85. SCr mg/dL = SCr µmol/L ÷ 88.4.

Use the weight and kidney-function metric specified by the relevant drug label/local protocol. Current NIDDK guidance notes that FDA does not mandate one estimating equation for all drugs and newer, more accurate eGFR equations should be considered when possible. Cockcroft–Gault remains useful where a product label specifically bases dose adjustment on CrCl.

NIDDK — Determining drug dosing in adults with CKD · FDA 2024 renal-impairment PK guidance

Adult eGFR — CKD-EPI 2021
eGFR mL/min/1.73 m²
Formula, recommendation & references

2021 CKD-EPI creatinine: eGFR = 142 × min(SCr/κ,1)α × max(SCr/κ,1)−1.200 × 0.9938Age × 1.012 if female; κ=0.7 female/0.9 male, α=−0.241 female/−0.302 male.

If cystatin C is entered: the calculator also uses the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine–cystatin C equation. Current NIDDK/KDIGO guidance prefers combined creatinine–cystatin C when available because it is more accurate, particularly near critical clinical/drug-dosing decision thresholds. Race coefficients are not used.

For drug dosing in markedly non-average body size, consider de-indexing eGFR: absolute GFR (mL/min) = indexed eGFR × BSA/1.73. Use the medicine's current label/local renal-dosing guidance.

NIDDK — Adult eGFR equations · KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline

Paediatric renal function — CKiD U25 eGFR
eGFR mL/min/1.73 m²
Formula, recommendation & references

CKiD U25 creatinine equation: eGFR = κ × [height(m) ÷ SCr(mg/dL)], where κ varies by age and sex. SCr mg/dL = SCr µmol/L ÷ 88.4.

NIDDK currently describes CKiD U25 as the preferred paediatric calculator because it has less bias across a broader age range than the older 2009 bedside Schwartz equation. For ages 18–25, compare the CKiD U25 and adult CKD-EPI estimates. If cystatin C is available, combined creatinine–cystatin C estimation is more accurate.

This is an eGFR estimate, not measured creatinine clearance.

Privacy

Patient data are requested from the password-protected Apps Script backend after the app is unlocked. The service worker caches only the PWA shell and medication-reference files; patient API responses are not intentionally stored in the offline cache.

Device-local settings such as appearance, default view, favourites and recently viewed medicines are stored in browser local storage. Locking the app clears the active backend session token from this browser session.

This shared-password design is intended for a controlled clinical environment. Follow institutional requirements for device security, patient confidentiality and access to identifiable clinical data.
